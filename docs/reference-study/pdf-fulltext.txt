How I Built a Portfolio That Hit 3 Lakh Views in 
2 days
Hey, if you're here that means you commented or DMed asking how this thing was built. 
So here's the real story, no fluff, and actually a lot more detail than what fit in the reel.
Quick disclaimer first
I'm not a coder or developer. I had my portfolio sitting as a PDF for a while and just 
wanted to try vibe coding, wasn't even sure it'd turn out decent. It did, and here we are.
How it actually started
Before touching any AI tool, I sat with the PDF and figured out how many sections I 
wanted, what story I wanted the portfolio to tell, and what vibe I was going for. Fonts, 
colors, overall tone, all decided manually first in Canva and Figma. AI mostly replicates 
what's already in your head, at least that's how it felt to me, it doesn't really invent taste 
on its own.
The full workflow
GPT: used this mostly for structuring, taking a raw idea and turning it into a rough 
section by section plan before any visual work started
Gemini: this is where most of the actual building happened, I'd feed it the structure 
plus visual references from Canva and Figma, and it would build the section out, got 
it right maybe 60 to 70 percent of the time on the first try
Antigravity and Claude: used after Gemini's first attempt, mainly for fixing bugs 
and cleaning up small things like spacing, little stuff I had to manually catch and fix 
along the way
Canva and Figma: this was the first step I took for pretty much every section, at 
least for me. the thing that made the biggest difference was taking the exact 
dimensions of the website section I was about to build, then designing that entire 
section in Canva or Figma to those exact dimensions, typography, shadows, boxes, 
spacing, even the images, basically a full mockup instead of just a rough 
moodboard. exported it as SVG or PNG and fed that directly to GPT and Gemini as 
reference. it just made things easier since AI had something exact to copy instead 
of guessing from a text description. this is just what worked for me though, not 
saying it's the only way to do it
Coder friend: brought in for a few things I genuinely got stuck on and couldn't 
figure out myself

Diary section
The core idea was control, you control what the viewer's eye lands on next, like a story 
unfolding rather than a static page. inspired by a diary and notes style post I'd made 
earlier on Instagram and LinkedIn, wanted to carry that visual language into the portfolio 
itself.
Stickers and sticky notes were added purely for personality, picked things that were 
actually me, favorite drinks, things off my own Pinterest board, so it felt personal instead 
of generic. that's the detail worth using if you try something similar, don't just add 
random decorative elements, tie them to things that are genuinely you.
Metro station section
Started as a much wider concept, a horizon line with the metro running across it on 
the metro highway or whatever we call it, caricatures below, Gurgaon style buildings 
on one end, older Delhi lanes on the other, hauz khas cafe. looked great as a concept 
but got way too cluttered and heavy once actually built, so it got trimmed down to the 
simpler version live now, just the metro and billboards.
If you're thinking of trying something similar, I'd just say maybe don't copy the metro 
idea exactly, that one was specific to me. the bigger idea was picking something the 
people you're sending it to would probably relate to, recruiters and clients have likely 
experienced something like a metro ride or metro food at some point, so it tends to land. 
this is just what I think worked, not a rule or anything.
Projects section
Kept this one intentionally simple compared to the rest, cards that expand on click, 
wanted the actual work to stand out without extra visual noise competing for attention.
Visuals section

The honest flip side
This portfolio isn't mobile friendly. used heavy GSAP animations without knowing much 
about media queries or web accessibility at the time, learnt that one the hard way. 
actively working on fixing it. If anyone is planning to make their portfolio from scratch, 
make sure to get familiar with media queries, web accessibility etc (prefer talking to 
someone who knows the tech stuff)

The rest of the scrapped ideas
(already shared the pin board idea in the free doc, here are the other four)
The vending machine
A vending machine sitting top center of the screen, a small display inside showing 
rotating fun text, a coin slot next to it. you drag a coin into the slot and a card, basically a 
case study card, gets dispensed out like an actual vending machine. this can work with 
any type of portfolio considering that the main catch is the visual design of the whole 
concept (you can make it comic type, tech style or wtvr you want to). You all may 
wonder but this inspiration came from doraemon episodes, haha
Scrapped mainly because the animation got too complex to pull off cleanly with AI, kept 
breaking on mobile or looking janky. also started feeling more tech product than creative 
direction, which didn't match the direction I wanted. If you can make it work in a better 
way, then its definitely gonna be something unique, maybe nostalgic as well.
The folder
A closed folder sitting on screen, hover over it and it opens, cards flying out in a blurred 
motion, each one a project you can click into for more detail.
Scrapped mostly as I thought it will not go with the diary section idea. It is something 
that is not going to give you a lot of problems with accessibility across screens, so 
definitely worth a try if you don’t want to go with heavy animations. However, it may not 
seem to very out of the box idea if you keep it simple.
The radio
You set different frequencies for different cards, dial into a specific frequency and a card 
pops up on screen along with a voiceover effect like an actual radio.

The illustrated room
Mainly for illustrators or people who draw manually. build out an entire room, your 
illsutrated character sitting at a table, laptop, a coffee or soft drink can, a clock on the 
wall, your tagline somewhere in the room, plus personal touches like a cat under the 
table if you're into cats, or books where each book represents a different case study, or 
frames on the wall each holding a different project.
Scrapped because it demands actual illustration skill and heavy animation work, not 
something AI handles well on its own. if someone has the drawing chops though, i 
personally believe this has a ton of creative freedom.

A few honest tips, zero gatekeeping
Putting these out fully instead of holding anything back, mainly because I don't see a 
real reason to gatekeep any of it. also want to be clear these are just things that worked 
for me, not hard rules, and I got plenty of this wrong along the way too.
1.  From what I've noticed, recruiters have very little time, maybe 15 to 20 seconds 
before they decide whether to keep looking. that's part of why I put heavier 
animation in the about section, since that's where the actual information starts.
2.  Something I did, and would suggest, is giving people a shortcut if they're short on 
time. in my projects section I left a note saying these are the projects that built my 
foundation, but if you want to see the work I'm actually proud of, skip ahead to the 
best work section. just a way to let people jump to your strongest section instead of 
scrolling through everything.
3.  I tried to keep my origin story simple and honest instead of dressing it up. mine was 
literally just wanting to afford sneakers, nothing dramatic, and I feel like that 
honesty is part of what made it relatable. 
4.  I've got a lot of projects listed too, so this one's a bit ironic coming from me, but I 
try to point people toward the ones I'm proudest of in case they don't have time to 
go through everything. just about giving people a choice instead of forcing a full 
scroll.
5.  This one's genuinely a mistake on my end. I didn't know much about media queries 
or web accessibility until a coder friend pointed it out, and by then I'd already built 
too much to fix easily. if you're hoping for this to be client ready and not just a 
personal experiment, it might help to loop in someone who knows development and 
getting familiar with the some tech stuff
6.  One thing I'd keep in mind is not making people click too many times just to get 
what they want. this was actually the flaw in my vending machine idea, you had to 
keep clicking just to see each case study. ideally someone should be able to jump 
to your fifth project without going through a repetitive loop.
7.  If there's something you think is valuable but don't want to shove in someone's 
face, maybe just let it be discoverable, something people find while hovering or 
exploring on their own, instead of forcing it into their eyeline. (for example, my 
insomniac work section)
8.  In my experience, AI mostly helped with building things out, not really coming up 
with the ideas themselves. I had to have the actual idea in my head first and make it 
on canva and figma, AI just helped structure and execute it. so having some sense 
of design yourself probably matters more than knowing any particular tool.
9.  There's a trade off between adding creativity to every little element and keeping the 
site light, I learned that one by doing a bit too much of the former. I got detail heavy 
on almost every button just because that's how I like working, and it's also what 
made the site heavy. maybe find your own balance, this is just where mine ended 
up.
