## A. From model to interface

A voice signal rises through capture, local inference and recoverable history until it becomes a tool someone can use. This follows the actual Fixo Flow workflow and expresses the span of Abrar’s work. The opening object is an authored diagram. The next scene is the actual macOS writing styles interface.

[Perceived ascent](http://127.0.0.1:5181/docs/v5/prototypes/ascent.html) · [Literal reverse](http://127.0.0.1:5181/docs/v5/prototypes/ascent.html?scroll=reverse) · [Desktop opening](evidence/directions/a-ascent/desktop-000.jpg) · [Mobile opening](evidence/directions/a-ascent/mobile-000.jpg) · [Desktop evidence scene](evidence/directions/a-ascent/desktop-100.jpg) · [Desktop motion](evidence/directions/a-ascent/desktop-motion.mp4) · [Mobile motion](evidence/directions/a-ascent/mobile-motion.mp4) · [Native upward recording](evidence/directions/a-reverse/desktop-motion.mp4)

Art direction. Bricolage Grotesque Bold sets the image-scale project statements, Instrument Sans Regular/Semibold handles the biography and controls, and DM Mono typesets the process stages. All three are SIL OFL 1.1, with licenses beside the existing local font files. In the prototype the display ranges from 46–120 px, body from 16–22 px, and diagram labels from 9–12 px. A 12-column system with approximately 5/7 hero proportions gives the graphics room and reduces to one reading column on mobile. Roughly 65% of the first two scenes is the cobalt and night family (`#202eb0`, `#2637c7`, `#121331`), 25% warm stock or sheet (`#f5f0e3`, `#fffaf0`), and 10% accent across pink, yellow, and lilac. Cool, graded light hits the sectional diagram. The project lands in neutral front light on backed printed stock. Use the real portrait as a small identity cue, actual interface captures as proof, and draw architecture from the existing project layers.

Signature. The yellow trace leads the eye across the layered object. Scroll displacement lifts the stack and offsets the ruled field. At the seam, three offset cut layers expose the actual UI. The transition borrows Cuetone’s idea of changing the frame around a recognizable subject. The prototype changes from graphic trace to print facsimile across the seam. A truly continuous trace into the interface remains a slice design problem.

Risks. The technical object must make sense without reading the diagram labels. Three.js and a background shader add GPU and battery risk. Test one context, inexpensive fallbacks and native touch before advancing beyond the slice. Literal reverse adds an orientation cue, atypical scrollbar position, and different visual versus DOM direction across sections. The heading and links stay in story order in the DOM. Mobile and reduced-motion use downward navigation in both variants. Native find, restoration and first-paint limitations are called out in the scorecard.

## B. The work behind the window

A portfolio unfolded as an engineer’s folio puts the reasoning next to the product. Framecast Studio’s landscape and portrait outputs give the hinged opening a specific subject, and the sheet’s published caption names the product before the visitor opens it.

[Open B](http://127.0.0.1:5181/docs/v5/prototypes/folio.html) · [Desktop opening](evidence/directions/b-folio/desktop-000.jpg) · [Mobile opening](evidence/directions/b-folio/mobile-000.jpg) · [Desktop evidence scene](evidence/directions/b-folio/desktop-100.jpg) · [Desktop motion](evidence/directions/b-folio/desktop-motion.mp4) · [Mobile motion](evidence/directions/b-folio/mobile-motion.mp4)

Art direction. Instrument Serif sets the author and case statements, Instrument Sans sets body and navigation, and DM Mono appears in figure captions. All are SIL OFL 1.1. Display use ranges from 34 to 164 px, prose from 16 to 18 px, and annotations from 11 to 13 px. The opening divides at a roughly 37/63 vertical hinge. The second scene uses a top header and central, horizontal proof spread. Roughly 40% cobalt, 45% cream/lilac, 10% night/ink, and 5% yellow or pink accents. The blue spine uses the extracted grid under a gradient mask. Raking hinge light changes with the angle, then flattens over the interface facsimile. Photography belongs to the author’s leaf and actual screenshots appear as prints with labels grounded in the product. A small proof print sits in the corner so a recruiter can infer what the first case is about.

Signature. The cover’s cast shadow deepens with scroll. The cream surface beneath it rotates upward and the actual Framecast screenshot settles toward a flat view. The width of the proof spread lets the two canvas frames remain visible. In a slice, align the outgoing thumbnail to the incoming print to strengthen continuity.

CSS perspective is small and inexpensive, but legibility must survive intermediate angles. Device-height changes can leave too much blank stock around the cover title.

## C. The patchbay

A visitor asks for a voice, HTTP request or screen signal and sees which of Abrar’s builds turns it into usable output. The relationship is derived from Fixo Flow, PacketLark and Framecast Studio. The board changes the case rather than just decorating it.

[Open C](http://127.0.0.1:5181/docs/v5/prototypes/patchbay.html) · [Desktop opening](evidence/directions/c-patchbay/desktop-000.jpg) · [Mobile opening](evidence/directions/c-patchbay/mobile-000.jpg) · [Desktop evidence scene](evidence/directions/c-patchbay/desktop-100.jpg) · [Desktop motion](evidence/directions/c-patchbay/desktop-motion.mp4) · [Mobile motion](evidence/directions/c-patchbay/mobile-motion.mp4)

Art direction. Instrument Sans Semibold is used from oversized headings to touch controls, with DM Mono for port labels and method states. Both are SIL OFL 1.1. Display ranges from 50–150 px, body from 16–20 px, and labels from 11–14 px. The hero crosses a cobalt diagonal into a three-column patch board. The evidence scene is a single full-width inspector, with a name and description adjoining the image inside the inspector. Roughly 50% night, 30% cobalt, 15% selected trace accent and 5% warm light type. The selected trace only uses existing lime, yellow or pink. The first scene puts cool light on the input board. The output scene brings coloured backlighting through a three-layer shutter. Real product UI replaces the abstract signal at the output and conditions the name, description, state and source link.

Signature. A keyboard, touch or pointer selection travels through an actual animated SVG connection, changes the output label, and preselects the named project in the scene below. As the visitor advances, the accent spans the whole scene and the real screenshot slides inside the inspector aperture. Try Request with Enter or tap Screen before using View project.

| Home scene | Desktop composition and transition | Mobile composition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Arrival and routing | Three equivalent ports, one visible route and one stable output slot. | Big buttons next to short traces and a stable output action. |
| Output inspector | Wide, dark dossier window against the selected trace color. | Details before the full-width actual screenshot. |
| Work matrix | Plain index lets recruiters scan the whole portfolio by problem or stack separately from routing. | Filterable list with stable project links. |
| Design decisions | Bento proof board rearranges around a chosen project decision. | Explicit selector expands one proof area in place. |
| The Neural Log | Reading lane uses low-contrast background texture and a straightforward search entry. | Open reading list with native controls. |
| About | Portrait and short timeline flow along a vertical conductor. | Portrait, dates and résumé in sequence. |
| Contact | Output becomes a full-width final step built around direct email and hiring links. | Single linked contact line and résumé. |

Risks. Port language can read as a technical demo at the expense of the portfolio’s first job. Work must remain directly reachable by name, the board must support keyboard and tap, and decisions and status must be visible outside the trace. Terminal Pulse is the closest legacy reference. Maintain direct manipulation and read-at-rest evidence without reproducing a terminal, prompt, command overlay, or console layout.
