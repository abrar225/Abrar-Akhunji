# Zero to Built: Abrar's Build Room

Refined concept direction, 28 October 2026. Supersedes the three-way exploration as the recommended narrative. This is a design specification, not an implemented website or a claim of validated performance. Storyboard images are art direction; application screens and portrait placements must be replaced with authentic assets.

## The central idea

A visitor enters Abrar's Build Room and sees how a useful problem becomes working software. The room's signature object is a small tabletop Build Line: an aluminium track that carries open, screen-bearing project trays across a workbench. Each tray holds a recognizable working product. Selecting a product brings its tray under an inspection light; opening it exposes the engineering and release evidence. The visitor eventually reaches an empty tray and an invitation to start the next build.

This is a metaphor for digital product creation. The website must immediately establish software/AI engineering so the physical scene is not mistaken for hardware manufacturing. The track does not imply that unrelated applications integrate with one another or that each application uses AI.

The user accepted the broad Build Room idea but found the previous alternatives insufficiently distinctive. The earlier room contained attractive objects without a strong action connecting them. This refinement gives the room a persistent activity, content-navigation mechanism and concluding callback.

## Positioning and exact opening copy

Visible name: Abrar Akhunji. Primary role: AI & Full-Stack Engineer. Supporting expertise: AI/ML. Product engineering. Deployment. The résumé and capability section retain the user's three target roles explicitly: AI Engineer, Full-Stack Engineer, AI/ML Engineer. Native desktop/media work adds evidence of range. DevOps is supported by concrete build, CI, packaging and deployment examples, with no unsupported claims of operational scale.

Hero heading: “Zero to built.” Supporting sentence: “I build intelligent products, from the model and application to the tested release.” If a featured project does not involve a model, its case study uses its own accurate lifecycle. Primary action: “Explore my work”. Secondary links: “Resume” and “Contact”. Hero must show a clear product preview rather than only physical props.

Brand character: curious, capable, resourceful and hands-on as expressed through the work. These are proposed brand attributes, not inferred private personality traits. Real portrait, location and optional rap/cooking details can come from the existing portfolio. Do not manufacture a dramatic origin story or new favourites.

## Why the object belongs

The Metro reference uses a familiar moving object as a project container. Our Build Line uses a workbench-scale moving object to explain the user's end-to-end engineering. Its small open product trays are visual containers for real software; the inspection light focuses attention; the opening tray connects the external product with its internal decisions; the release receipt makes delivery visible; the last empty tray invites collaboration.

The reference's story principles and the supplied guide inform this adaptation. We are taking continuity, physical navigation, selective disclosure, shortcuts and a calm ending. We are not combining its Metro, diary, vending machine, radio and wallet into another multi-theme site. The guide's abandoned concepts are source material, not instructions to reproduce them. It explicitly describes the costs of clutter, mobile weaknesses and repetitive interactions.

## The six-scene story

### 1. Arrive at the bench

User question answered: Who is Abrar, what does he build, and where is the work?

Compose a readable left-aligned introduction with a low, wide tabletop line on the right and below. A rough paper brief rests at one end; product screens farther along the track establish the transformation. The name and role are visible immediately. The track is the memorable silhouette. Avoid a generic laptop-on-desk photograph or a full-screen room that hides the headline.

Motion: one restrained first-view assembly, approximately 600–900 ms, settles the headline, task light and first tray. This is not a blocking preloader or a pretend asset-loading counter. No automatic endless conveyor movement. The first project and navigation are usable before decorative motion completes.

Exit: the paper's drawn line aligns with the physical track as the page moves toward the brief. Content stays in normal vertical document order. “Explore my work” skips directly to scene 3.

### 2. Find a useful problem

User question answered: Why do these projects exist?

Use a loose working brief clipped to the same bench, not a copied diary. Heading: “It starts with a useful problem.” Three concrete prompts introduce the newer projects: Fixo Flow, speaking into everyday applications; PacketLark, composing and testing API requests; Framecast Studio, composing and recording screen/camera output. Show one line per problem with a product name. This introduces purpose before architecture. Keep it short enough that it does not become a gate before the strongest work.

Motion: a drawn line continues onto the track as the composition shifts from a wide introduction to the product inspection view. Use one shared transition across approximately 0.6–0.9 viewport of normal scrolling. Values are prototype targets. This scene can be a short transitional band rather than a second oversized hero.

### 3. Choose a product on the Build Line

User question answered: What has he actually built?

Heading: “Built to be used.” The main scene shows three open product trays with distinct software previews. Fixo Flow has a waveform and English transcript example; PacketLark shows an HTTP request and response; Framecast shows landscape and portrait composition previews and an “In development” label. This is the signature section, analogous in function to the reference's Metro showcase.

Make one tray prominent under an articulated inspection lamp. Adjacent trays remain partly visible to suggest breadth. Above the scene, ordinary labelled buttons list all featured projects; visitors can select any project immediately. A fourth applied-ML entry must remain readily visible in an adjacent “AI/ML studies” link or ordinary project list. The illustrated line does not need endless trays for the entire archive.

A project selection changes only the selected tray, light direction, title, one-sentence problem, role and links. The tray translates a short distance to the inspection point over approximately 350–500 ms. Its screen uses a brief 150–250 ms crossfade. An interruption redirects from the current visual position rather than queueing animations. Never rotate through projects automatically while someone is reading.

“View project” opens its actual public site/repository where appropriate; “Read the build” opens a shareable case-study URL. Framecast uses a local-demo video or case study while unpublished. Fixo Flow's website/release is not an embedded browser dictation application. PacketLark's GitHub Pages site is not a hosted Node workbench; a portfolio preview should be a labelled recording or sample. External applications load only on explicit intent. No microphone, camera, recording, OAuth or download is triggered by exploring the portfolio.

No coins, mandatory dragging, timing game, repeated dispensing loop or forced traversal of earlier projects. Arrow keys may be supported when the selector has focus, with ordinary buttons always available. Vertical scrolling remains vertical. A short optional pin of at most one viewport is only retained if the prototype benefits; the default is a normal section with user-triggered tray motion.

### 4. Inspect the build

User question answered: Can he explain and deliver the engineering?

Heading: “What made it work.” The selected tray opens slightly and its software layers separate beside a readable case-study summary. The artwork illustrates the structure; selectable labels and explanatory text stay HTML. The brief should make the contribution, decisions and limitations easy to find.

Map real architecture per project. PacketLark can expose interface, HTTP engine and workspace files, with the isolated test-script layer discussed in its full study. Fixo Flow can expose capture, local speech inference and OS insertion/history. Framecast can expose scene composition, media capture/mix and encoded outputs. Do not show an AI layer on PacketLark or Framecast just to match the overall positioning.

A small “Build receipt” shows sourced evidence: tests, package/build path, distribution/deployment and current status. It is a structured summary, not a fake terminal log or green tick wallpaper. Link to the supporting code, workflow, release or measured result. Distinguish configured CI from a verified successful current run, local builds from published releases, and application hosting from static marketing sites. Any unavailable metric is omitted.

Motion: selecting “Inside the build” or opening a case study produces one controlled cutaway over roughly 400–600 ms. Reading is stationary. The complete case study is a regular page, not text trapped inside a rotating 3D machine. The same tray identity carries through the transition and gives a clear way back to work.

### 5. Meet the builder and explore further

User question answered: Who is this person, how did they develop, and what else can I inspect?

Heading: “The person behind the builds.” Use the existing authentic portrait as a strong editorial image. A short honest personal introduction connects engineering interests with real context. Experience and education remain clear, scan-friendly entries. Resolve the existing 2024 internship date conflict before publishing rewritten history.

Keep “AI/ML”, “Full-stack” and “Delivery” as three evidence-backed capability groups rather than an unstructured wall of logos. Each group links to a relevant project or engineering explanation. A low side shelf presents the older archive: FixO CLI, GourmetGo, vision studies and other existing projects. All remain reachable through ordinary links and project URLs. Selected writing appears as two or three real field notes with an obvious full-blog link. Preserve existing blog paths.

Personal details are sparse discoveries: a rap notebook annotation, a recipe margin note only if the user wants it, location and real portrait. Use them to humanize the builder; do not add stock coffee, cats, motivational quotes, favorite artists or academic trophies that have no source. This scene provides relief from the machine's detail. Motion is limited to selecting/opening actual content.

### 6. Bring the next brief

User question answered: How can we work together?

Heading: “What should we build next?” The same track ends beside an empty tray and a fresh brief sheet. The visual callback completes the story: the work the visitor saw began as a problem, and the next project could begin here. Keep a large real email link, résumé, GitHub and relevant availability. All contact content stays visible, readable and selectable. No fake commissioning UI or interaction is required to reveal the email.

Motion: the scene settles; the empty tray does not continuously beckon or animate. Sound remains off by default throughout. An optional gentle click is only enabled after explicit opt-in. The ending should be quieter than the project showcase, following the reference guide's useful restraint.

## Purposeful visual elements

The aluminium track provides spatial continuity and short project-selection movement. The open screen trays contain recognizable software and avoid anonymous branded boxes. The task light supplies selection feedback. The paper brief explains a real problem. The cutaway explains decisions. The release receipt proves delivery. The side shelf preserves breadth. The portrait introduces the person. The empty final tray gives contact a narrative payoff. These are the complete core elements: additions need an informational or navigational job.

## Locked art direction for the prototype

Mineral white #EEF1ED is the dominant page material; aluminium #BAC4C8 carries the physical object; navy ink #243749 carries text and quieter dark surfaces; oxide red #A53E35 identifies the track rollers and key actions; amber #E8B85C is a small inspection/selection cue. Keep neutral daylight rather than the earlier heavy sepia desk atmosphere. Product brand colors belong inside authentic product screens and should not recolor the whole room per selection. Accessible UI text/focus colors require implementation checks; physical material colors are not automatically suitable text colors.

Use an expressive grotesk for headings and a restrained readable sans for body text; prototype Bricolage Grotesque with Instrument Sans before finalizing. Monospace belongs in request/code examples and genuine technical values. Avoid handwritten large headings copied from the reference; use a little handwriting only on the brief/notes. Keep headings aligned to content rather than lettering baked into an image. Set readable copy line lengths near 55–75 characters.

Desktop composition is left-aligned with a broad track, generous reading space and a single dominant object. Do not tile every section into the same rounded-card grid. The track can cross an edge deliberately, but text and controls stay within the readable layout. Use light/dark contrast for the inspection scene where it supports product content, not a different visual brand in each section.

## Mobile, keyboard and motion behavior

The narrative and content stay the same on mobile. A compact frontal tray replaces the wide track; direct project-name buttons wrap into sensible rows; its description and links appear below. The track becomes a static horizontal detail or vertical connector, not a horizontally scrolling page. The cutaway becomes a labelled vertical diagram. Archive, writing, history and contact use readable document layouts. No tiny desktop machine is scaled down to phone size.

All controls use semantic elements, visible focus, descriptive names and usable touch targets. A native cursor remains reliable. Selection state is conveyed in text and aria state as well as light/color. Reduced motion removes camera, line drawing, pins, parallax and mechanical translation while preserving every action. Sound has no informational role. No important content depends on hover, drag, animation completion or WebGL support.

## Asset and build contract

Create one master bench/track object with a consistent camera, lamp and tray proportions. Derive the wide hero, inspection composition and mobile frontal crop from that master. Prefer separated render layers and SVG/HTML controls for the first prototype. A pre-rendered object plus real screen inserts can achieve the intended look without making the entire portfolio a 3D world. Reserve runtime 3D for interaction that earns its loading/rendering cost.

Required authentic assets: existing Abrar portrait; Fixo Flow English dictation recording/screens; PacketLark request/test capture; Framecast dual-output preview and development label; applied-ML sample with real evidence; existing archive thumbnails; selected actual articles; verified résumé/contact/availability. Illustrative storyboard UI does not substitute for this evidence.

One motion owner controls each element's transform. Scope the signature choreography to hero/work; keep reading scenes ordinary. Load one main artwork first and lazy-load detailed media; stop offscreen work. Use a static preview and native HTML navigation if motion support fails. Do not promise a performance score before measuring the prototype.

## Implementation sequence and acceptance

The next implementation is a vertical slice: hero, brief transition, Build Line selector and one real project case summary, plus mobile/reduced-motion variants. After judging composition and interaction on a real phone and desktop, extend the same world to evidence, archive and contact. Do not rebuild every page before proving the signature object.

Acceptance means visitors can immediately identify Abrar's profession, reach any featured project without a repetitive sequence, distinguish released/preview/in-development work, understand one significant engineering decision and find contact/resume directly. Keyboard and reduced-motion paths must expose the same content. Animation should follow intent and remain interruptible. Test with actual assets before locking motion timings or payload targets.
